# OctoAcme Personas

This document defines typical roles and responsibilities used in OctoAcme project docs and exercises.

---

## Developers

### Role Summary
Developers design, build, test, and deliver software components. They collaborate with product and project leads to implement features that meet acceptance criteria and quality standards.

### Responsibilities
- Implement features and fixes to meet acceptance criteria
- Write and maintain tests and documentation
- Participate in design and code reviews
- Assist in estimating and planning work
- Help identify technical risks and propose mitigations

### Goals
- Deliver reliable, maintainable code
- Reduce cycle time from idea to production
- Maintain high test coverage and observability

### Typical Communication
- Daily standups and sprint planning
- PR descriptions and code review comments
- Technical design docs when needed

---

## Product Managers

### Role Summary
Product Managers define what should be built to deliver customer and business value. They own the product vision, prioritize the backlog, and measure outcomes.

### Responsibilities
- Define problem statements and success metrics
- Prioritize the roadmap and backlog
- Collaborate with stakeholders and engineering on trade-offs
- Validate solutions through user research and metrics

### Goals
- Maximize customer value and impact
- Make clear, data-driven prioritization decisions
- Ensure product-market fit and usability

### Typical Communication
- Weekly alignment with PM and engineering leads
- Roadmap updates and stakeholder briefings
- Acceptance criteria and feature specs

---

## Project Managers

### Role Summary
Project Managers coordinate delivery activities, manage schedules, risks, and communications. They enable the team to deliver on commitments efficiently.

### Responsibilities
- Create and maintain project plans and timelines
- Manage risks, dependencies, and resource constraints
- Facilitate meetings (kickoff, planning, retrospectives)
- Ensure consistent project documentation and status reporting
- Coordinate cross-team and stakeholder communication

### Goals
- Deliver projects on time and within scope
- Minimize unplanned work and escalations
- Maintain transparency and alignment across stakeholders

### Typical Communication
- Weekly status updates and stakeholder reports
- Risk registers and decision logs
- Coordination via project boards and meeting facilitation

---

## UX Designers / User Researchers

### Role Summary
UX Designers and User Researchers investigate user needs and shape intuitive workflows and interfaces. They turn research and product direction into designs that the team can validate and implement.

### Responsibilities
- Plan and conduct user research, and share findings with the team
- Create workflows, wireframes, prototypes, and design specifications
- Validate usability and iterate designs based on feedback
- Identify accessibility and experience risks early

### Goals
- Ensure solutions address real user needs
- Make workflows clear, accessible, and usable
- Give the team validated designs before implementation

### Typical Communication
- Research findings and user journey documentation
- Design reviews, prototypes, and usability test results
- Design specifications and handoff discussions with Developers

### Interactions with Existing Roles
- **Product Managers:** Use research findings to inform product priorities, requirements, and acceptance criteria; validate proposed solutions against user needs.
- **Developers:** Explain design intent and specifications, answer implementation questions, and review the built experience for consistency with the design.
- **Project Managers:** Coordinate research and design milestones, surface dependencies, and communicate risks that could affect delivery.

---

## QA / Test Engineers

### Role Summary
QA / Test Engineers plan and carry out quality validation so the team can understand whether the product behaves as expected and is ready to release.

### Responsibilities
- Define a test strategy based on product risks and acceptance criteria
- Design, automate where appropriate, and execute tests
- Report and help prioritize defects, then verify fixes
- Communicate coverage, quality findings, and release risks

### Goals
- Find important issues early and reduce regressions
- Provide clear evidence of product quality and remaining risk
- Ensure behavior meets agreed acceptance criteria

### Typical Communication
- Test plans, test results, and defect reports
- Testability and defect-triage discussions with Developers
- Quality-readiness updates and risk assessments

### Interactions with Existing Roles
- **Developers:** Coordinate on testability, test coverage, defect investigation, and verification of fixes.
- **Product Managers:** Confirm expected behavior against acceptance criteria and clarify product questions or trade-offs revealed by testing.
- **Project Managers:** Align test milestones with the delivery plan and report quality risks or blockers that may affect release dates.

---

## DevOps / Release Engineers

### Role Summary
DevOps / Release Engineers help teams build, deploy, and operate software reliably. They support the delivery infrastructure and operational readiness needed to release and monitor changes.

### Responsibilities
- Maintain or improve build, deployment, and delivery pipelines
- Support environment reliability and release processes
- Help establish monitoring and operational readiness
- Identify infrastructure and release risks, and support rollback planning

### Goals
- Make releases repeatable, reliable, and observable
- Reduce deployment friction and operational incidents
- Ensure the service is supportable after launch

### Typical Communication
- Pipeline, environment, and deployment documentation
- Release checklists and operational-readiness reviews
- Monitoring, incident, and rollout updates

### Interactions with Existing Roles
- **Developers:** Coordinate pipeline and environment needs, deployment configuration, monitoring, and operational handoff.
- **Product Managers:** Explain rollout options and operational impacts that may affect product decisions or customer communication.
- **Project Managers:** Align release plans, dependencies, and readiness milestones; escalate operational risks that could affect delivery.

---

## Security / Privacy Leads

### Role Summary
Security / Privacy Leads advise the team on protecting systems and data. They identify relevant requirements and risks and guide appropriate mitigations throughout design, implementation, and release.

### Responsibilities
- Identify security and privacy requirements, threats, and risks
- Advise on mitigations and review relevant designs or release decisions
- Help Developers apply secure implementation practices
- Escalate material risks and communicate unresolved concerns

### Goals
- Protect users, data, and systems
- Address security and privacy risks early
- Make risk decisions and mitigations visible to the team

### Typical Communication
- Security and privacy requirements, reviews, and risk assessments
- Mitigation guidance and follow-up findings
- Escalations through project risk and decision processes

### Interactions with Existing Roles
- **Developers:** Collaborate on secure design and implementation, review mitigations, and track remediation of findings.
- **Product Managers:** Clarify security and privacy requirements and explain risks or trade-offs that may affect product scope and acceptance.
- **Project Managers:** Record and escalate material risks, coordinate review and mitigation work, and make unresolved release concerns visible in delivery decisions.

---

## Role Assignment and Decision Ownership
- These personas describe responsibilities, not necessarily separate positions. On smaller projects, one person may cover multiple roles, but each responsibility should have a clear owner.
- Product Managers own product priorities and acceptance; Project Managers coordinate delivery, risks, and escalation.
- UX, QA, DevOps / Release, and Security / Privacy specialists advise and execute within their areas, making their findings and handoffs visible to the team.
